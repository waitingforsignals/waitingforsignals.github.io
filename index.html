The problem

Every weekday, about 700 trains squeeze through one corner.

Tower 18, at Lake and Wells, is “the busiest section of track in the entire CTA system” (CTA, Oct 2025) and “one of the busiest rail interlockings in the nation” (CTA, Oct 2026). When trains have to cross each other's paths there, someone waits. You've heard the announcement: “We are standing momentarily, waiting for signal clearance” (CTA).

  • ~700trains through Tower 18 on an average weekdaySource: CTA, Oct 2025
  • 2lines that circle the whole Loop in opposite directions: Brown one way, Orange the otherSources: CTA Brown Line, CTA Orange Line
  • “At or near”capacity: CTA's own description of the Inner LoopSource: CTA Rail Capacity Study, p.7

CTA's capacity study names the fixes: “adjusting Loop routing patterns” and “simplifying movements at junctions” (CTA Rail Capacity Study, p.7). It’s the same kind of problem CTA spent big to fix at Belmont, where “Red, Purple, and Brown line trains must wait for each other to pass through the junction” (CTA/FTA, 2015). This proposal is about how CTA routes trains, nothing more.

The fix

Run Brown and Orange as one line.

Kimball → Merchandise Mart → Wells → Van Buren → Roosevelt → Midway

And back the same way. No loop-the-Loop. No more Orange Line left turn from Lake onto Wells at Tower 18.

  • Green: stays on Lake and Wabash, unchanged.
  • Pink and Purple Express: keep circling the Loop.
  • East-Loop riders: transfer at Roosevelt or Merchandise Mart.

What riders get

Less waiting. More reach.

Fewer crossings at Tower 18

Orange stops turning left from Lake onto Wells at Tower 18, across other lines' paths. CTA’s study says “simplifying movements at junctions increases trains possible on Loop” (CTA Rail Capacity Study, p.7). How much? Not yet measured; CTA should publish the results once it runs.

One seat, North Side to Midway

Ravenswood, Lincoln Square and Lakeview get a straight ride to the Southwest Side and Midway Airport. CTA already runs a few weekday rush trips that carry on from Orange to Brown and back: 4 each way (CTA Brown Line timetable).

Takes pressure off Kimball Yard

CTA’s capacity study says “Kimball Yard does not have space to store more trains, limiting the number of trains that can be operated on the Brown Line” (CTA Rail Capacity Study, p.7). Through-running makes sharing Midway Yard routine.

Green Line untouched

Green keeps its route on Lake and Wabash. Green riders lose nothing.

Easy transfers for the east Loop

Heading to Wabash or State? Change at Roosevelt or Merchandise Mart. It's a real tradeoff, so we're asking CTA to time the transfers there.

The price tag

CTA built a flyover for one junction. This one needs a schedule.

$320M

Reported cost of the Belmont flyover (Red-Purple Bypass), built to fix one junction conflict (NBC Chicago; Chicago Sun-Times). It's part of CTA's $2.1B RPM Phase One (CTA).

Low-cost

Through-running Brown and Orange is an operations-only fix: new schedules, signs and training. No new track. There are still some operating costs, and no one has published a figure, but it is a different scale entirely.

Fairness

The Southwest Side deserves a better link too.

Our view: CTA is putting $492M into “Your New Blue” on the O'Hare branch (CTA). That's a worthwhile investment. But North Side riders still can't reach Midway without a transfer. Midway and the Southwest Side should get a better connection too, and this one is mostly a scheduling change.

FAQ

Straight answers.

Will I lose my ride to the east side of the Loop?

If you ride Brown or Orange to Washington/Wabash, State/Lake or Adams/Wabash, you'd transfer at Roosevelt or Merchandise Mart. That's a real tradeoff, so we're asking CTA to time the transfers there.

Why the west and south sides?

Running along Wells and Van Buren links Kimball and Midway directly. It removes the Orange Line's left turn from Lake onto Wells at Tower 18 and the full-Loop trip for both lines, and Green stays on Lake and Wabash.

Is it free?

Free of construction, yes: it needs no new track, and CTA already runs a few through trains between Kimball and Midway (CTA Brown Line timetable, eff. Jan 5, 2026). Running it all day still has some operating costs, like new signs, schedules and operator training. No source we know of puts a dollar figure on it.

Does it add capacity?

It could free up capacity. Nobody has quantified the gain publicly yet. We're asking CTA to publish the Tower 18 and Tower 12 results when it runs.

Does CTA already run trains like this?

Yes, a few. The current Brown Line timetable shows 4 weekday trips each way that run through between the Orange and Brown lines (CTA Brown Line timetable). Riders call them “tiger trains” (Streetsblog Chicago), and CTA said Kimball Yard “simply doesn’t have enough capacity” for the full morning rush.

Is this an official CTA plan?

No. Waiting for Signals is an independent rider campaign. The maps on this site are unofficial and are not CTA products.
